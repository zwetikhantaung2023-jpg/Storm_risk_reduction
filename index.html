Causes of Storms

01 - Moisture

Moisture in the air usually comes from oceans through evaporation. Water vapour adds moisture to the air and helps form clouds. As warm air rises and cools, the water vapour condenses into tiny droplets, forming storm clouds. Moisture provides the water needed for heavy rain and thunderstorms.

02 - Lift

Lift is the upward movement of warm air that helps thunderstorms develop. It happens when warmer, less dense air rises above cooler, denser air. As the air rises higher, it cools and the moisture condenses into clouds. This process allows thunderstorm clouds to grow and may produce heavy rain.

03 - Wind Shear

Wind shear is a change in wind speed or direction at different heights in the atmosphere. It can affect the movement and development of thunderstorms. Strong wind shear can help storms become more organised and last longer, increasing the possibility of severe weather.

04 - Atmospheric Instability

Atmospheric instability occurs when warm, moist air is near the ground and cold, dry air is above it. The warm air expands, becomes less dense and rises, while cooler, denser air sinks. This movement creates convection, allowing storm clouds to grow taller and stronger.

Impact of the Storm

Property Damage

High winds, heavy rainfall, buildings destroyed, roads damaged, estimated economic losses, flood damage, electrical systems and agricultural property.

Environmental Impact

Areas of farmland damaged, trees uprooted, water pollution, soil erosion, wildlife habitat destruction, coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion and waste accumulation.

Community Disruption

Villages affected, schools closed, transportation problems, food and water shortages, communication failures, power disruptions and emergency service difficulties.

Human Health & Safety

Physical injuries, drowning risk, lack of clean water, interrupted medical services, food shortages and emotional stress.

Why Does Reducing Storm Risk Matter?

Reduce Damage

Use strong buildings, clear drains to prevent flooding, plant trees to help protect soil, and secure outdoor objects before storms to reduce storm damage.

Protect Lives

Follow weather warnings, create family emergency plans, keep emergency contacts available, prepare emergency kits, stay indoors during strong winds and move to higher ground during flood risks.

Support Local Communities

Share reliable storm information with families, educate families about storm risks, help communities prepare for storms, and provide food, clean water, and shelter after storms.
