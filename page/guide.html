Pathein Cyclone

The cyclone caused extensive damage in Pathein and other affected areas. Strong winds damaged buildings and property, while coastal communities were exposed to dangerous storm conditions. Combined losses across the affected areas reached around 10 million kyats. Four deaths were recorded in Yangon.

Pathein District Cyclone

The cyclone caused serious damage in Pathein District, with losses reported at more than 100 million kyats. Homes, buildings, and other property were affected, creating difficulties for residents and disrupting local livelihoods. Although the storm made landfall near Kyaukphyu, its impacts also reached Pathein District.

Pathein Cyclone

The cyclone caused major destruction in Pathein, resulting in 303 deaths, the loss of 10,190 cattle, and the destruction of 246,700 houses. Reported losses reached 776.5 million kyats, and a storm surge of around 3 feet was recorded. The destruction of homes, livestock, and property severely affected residents' safety and livelihoods.

Mala Cyclone

Cyclone Mala brought powerful winds of around 210 km/h and a significant storm surge to the coastal area near Gwa. The surge reached around 4.57 feet, creating a serious threat to coastal communities and low-lying land. Strong winds, heavy rainfall, and rising water levels affected nearby areas and damaged property.

Ayeyarwady Cyclone Nargis

Cyclone Nargis devastated the Ayeyarwady Delta, causing approximately 138,000 deaths and missing persons. Powerful winds and a destructive storm surge swept through coastal and delta communities, destroying villages, homes, and infrastructure. Farmland and livestock were severely affected, while many survivors lost their homes and sources of income. Communities also faced major shortages of safe water, food, shelter, and essential services.

Cyclonic Storm Komen

Cyclonic Storm Komen brought torrential rainfall that contributed to widespread flooding in Myanmar, including the Ayeyarwady Delta. In affected delta communities, floodwater submerged rice fields, cut off road transportation, and forced residents to travel by boat. Prolonged flooding damaged crops and livelihoods, displaced people, and made it difficult for communities to return to normal activities.

Cyclone Mocha

Cyclone Mocha made landfall near Sittwe, Rakhine State, on 14 May 2023. It brought extremely strong winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surges. The cyclone damaged homes, roads, power lines, and other infrastructure. Coastal communities were especially affected by flooding and strong winds.

Myanmar's Storm Vulnerability

Myanmar is exposed to several storm-related hazards, including tropical cyclones, strong winds, heavy rainfall, flooding and landslides.

Coastal regions are particularly exposed to cyclones and storm surges, while heavy rainfall can cause flooding and landslides in other parts of the country.

Did You Know?

Myanmar's cyclone season commonly includes April to May and October to November.

NATURAL DISASTERS

Understanding of Storm and Its Consequences

A storm is a natural weather event that brings strong winds, heavy rain, thunder, and lightning. Storms can happen in different forms, such as thunderstorms, tropical storms, and cyclones.

Consequences of Storms

Damage to houses and buildings
Flooding and destruction of roads
Damage to crops and farmland
Power cuts and communication problems
Harm to people, animals, and the environment
Understanding storms helps us prepare for natural disasters and protect ourselves and our families.

How to Prepare for a Thunderstorm

Storm Safety Team · Preparedness

Prepare before the storm

Thunderstorms can bring lightning, heavy rain, strong winds, and hail. Check trusted local weather updates and make sure everyone in your household knows where to shelter.

  • Charge phones and keep a flashlight and first-aid kit nearby.
  • Bring loose outdoor items inside.
  • Choose a sturdy indoor room away from windows.

During the storm

  • Go indoors when you hear thunder and stay inside until at least 30 minutes after the last thunder.
  • Stay away from windows, doors, plumbing, and corded electrical devices.
  • If you are outside and cannot reach a building, move away from open fields, isolated trees, and water, and seek a substantial shelter as soon as possible.

Preparing for a Tropical Cyclone

Storm Safety Team · Tropical Weather

Prepare before the cyclone

Tropical cyclones can cause damaging winds, flooding, and dangerous coastal conditions. Monitor official forecasts early, learn your local evacuation routes, and decide where your household will meet if separated.

  • Pack drinking water, ready-to-eat food, medicines, a flashlight, batteries, and a first-aid kit.
  • Keep important documents in a waterproof bag and charge phones and power banks.
  • Bring in loose outdoor items and follow local evacuation guidance.

During the cyclone

  • Stay indoors in a sturdy shelter, away from windows, and keep listening to official updates.
  • Do not go outside during a lull; dangerous winds can return.
  • If told to evacuate, leave promptly using recommended routes. Never enter floodwater.

Staying Safe During Monsoon Flooding

Storm Safety Team · Flood Safety

Prepare for heavy rain

Monsoon rain can quickly flood roads, homes, and low-lying areas. Follow local flood alerts, identify higher ground and safe routes, and keep medicines and important documents ready to take with you.

During flooding

  • Move to higher ground when water rises and follow evacuation instructions.
  • Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater. It may be deeper or faster than it looks and can hide hazards.
  • Keep away from fallen power lines and report them to local authorities.
  • If trapped by rising water, move to the highest safe level and contact emergency services.

Getting Ready for a Snowstorm

Storm Safety Team · Winter Weather

Prepare for cold and travel disruptions

Snowstorms can reduce visibility, make roads slippery, and cause power outages. Check forecasts, prepare warm clothing, food, water, medicines, and a flashlight, and avoid travel when officials advise against it.

During the snowstorm

  • Stay indoors and use several dry layers to keep warm.
  • Use only safe indoor heating. Never use a barbecue, camp stove, or fuel-powered generator indoors or in an enclosed space because of carbon monoxide danger.
  • If you must travel, tell someone your route, check road conditions, and bring an emergency kit.
  • When it is safe, check on neighbors who may need assistance.

Build a Family Storm Emergency Kit

Storm Safety Team · Preparedness

Gather what your household needs

A ready-to-carry kit can help your household manage if you need to shelter at home or leave quickly. Store it where everyone can find it and adapt it for children, older adults, pets, and anyone with medical needs.

  • Drinking water and shelf-stable food, plus a manual can opener.
  • Essential medicines, first-aid supplies, and copies of important documents in a waterproof bag.
  • Flashlight, spare batteries, battery-powered radio, charged power bank, and a whistle.
  • Warm clothes, sturdy shoes, hygiene items, cash, and basic supplies for pets.

Check it regularly

Replace expired food, water, and medicines, and make sure batteries and devices still work.

What to Do During Any Severe Storm

Storm Safety Team · During the Storm

Protect yourself and follow official alerts

Storm conditions can change quickly. Follow instructions from local emergency services and use a reliable radio or charged phone for updates. Keep your household together in a safe place whenever possible.

  • Shelter indoors, away from windows, and do not go outside to watch or film the storm.
  • Keep away from floodwater, damaged buildings, and fallen wires.
  • If officials tell you to evacuate, leave promptly and use the recommended route.
  • Call your local emergency number for immediate danger or urgent medical help.

After the storm, wait for an official all-clear before going outside or returning to an evacuated area.
