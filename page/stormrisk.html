Storm Risk & Preparedness

Understanding storm warnings and possible hazards can help individuals and families make safer decisions. Learn the warning stages, recognise potential risks, and prepare before severe weather arrives.

01

Understanding Threat Levels

Myanmar's official cyclone warning colour stages.

The Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH) uses different colour stages to communicate the development and movement of cyclones. These stages help authorities and the public understand the storm situation and prepare for possible impacts.

YELLOW STAGE
Storm Formation

A storm begins to form in the Bay of Bengal or Andaman Sea. People should start paying attention to weather forecasts and official announcements.

ORANGE STAGE
Storm Approaching

The storm is moving towards the Myanmar coastline. Residents should follow updated forecasts, review emergency plans, and prepare necessary supplies.

RED STAGE
Coastal Impact Expected

This stage covers the period around 12 hours before the storm approaches and crosses the Myanmar coast. Residents should follow official warnings and evacuation instructions.

BROWN STAGE
Storm Crossing the Coast

The storm is crossing the Myanmar coastline. Residents should remain in a safe shelter, avoid unnecessary travel, and continue following official weather announcements.

GREEN CONDITION
Storm Danger Has Passed

The storm has weakened and the danger has passed according to the official announcement. Residents should still wait for authorities to confirm that affected areas are safe before returning.

Important: These colour stages describe the cyclone situation. Always check the latest DMH announcements and follow local authorities' instructions. Do not assume that an area is safe just because the weather becomes calm.
02

Know the Risks

Understand the possible consequences of severe storms.

Storms can affect different areas in different ways. Coastal communities may face storm surges, while inland and low-lying areas may experience heavy rainfall, flooding, and strong winds. Understanding these hazards helps people recognise the risks in their surroundings.

Strong Winds

Powerful winds can damage houses, break tree branches, destroy crops, and cause objects to become dangerous when blown around.

Heavy Rainfall

Continuous heavy rainfall can cause rivers to overflow, flood residential areas, damage farmland, and interrupt transportation.

Storm Surge

Storm surges push seawater towards coastal areas. They can flood homes, damage buildings, and affect communities near the coastline.

Flash Floods

Sudden flooding can happen after intense rainfall, especially near rivers, low-lying land, and drainage areas. It can damage homes and make roads unsafe.

Landslides

Heavy rainfall can make slopes unstable and increase the possibility of soil movement, particularly in hilly and mountainous regions.

Infrastructure Damage

Storms can damage roads, bridges, electricity lines, and communication systems, making it harder for residents to receive assistance.
